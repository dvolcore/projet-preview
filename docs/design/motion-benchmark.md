# Motion-design comparison and implemented improvements

Reviewed September 5, 2026. Qualitative review of live pages, interface text and rendered browser views. No conversion, performance or nationwide-ranking claims are inferred from appearance.

| Reference | Observed strengths | Application to Pro Jet |
|---|---|---|
| [Lusion](https://lusion.co/) | Large contained 3D storytelling stage, clear headline, visible contact/menu controls, explicit scroll invitation and a separate reel action. | Keep the service message readable; give each chapter its own visual treatment instead of relying on one repeated hero. Four background placements and a guided service finder are now implemented. |
| [Active Theory](https://activetheory.net/) | Atmospheric particle/reflective environment, minimal Work/Contact navigation and a clear scroll cue. Its text-only response requires JavaScript. | Use atmospheric depth around content without making service information depend on a 3D renderer. The new effects are CSS layers behind ordinary HTML, paused offscreen and when the page is hidden. |
| [Bruno Simon](https://bruno-simon.com/) | A distinctive interactive world with an explicit start affordance. Published controls include audio, quality, map, keyboard/mobile/gamepad interaction and recovery options. | Make controls and next actions obvious. Pro Jet keeps direct call/quote routes available, offers Motion/Sound controls, and adds short Back/Continue/Start-over guides rather than requiring a game to contact the business. |

The source sites are creative-experience benchmarks. This review does not establish how their designs convert service customers or how Pro Jet ranks in search.

## What Pro Jet now does well

- A recognizable hero/monster identity and consistent navy, blue, steel, white and red palette.
- Arrival film plays once and holds; optional effects remain separately controllable.
- Motion is attached to useful actions: service exploration, real-data refreshes, charts and intake guidance.
- Phone access and real service links remain direct. Customer and CRM guides collect no personal information and do not invent appointments or diagnoses.

## Added in this pass

Website: Hydro currents at symptoms; Steel light at pricing; City embers at service area; Diagnostic grid at the service finder.

Dashboard: Telemetry grid at the signal desk; Signal orbits at connections; Receipt currents at activity; Readiness light at the guided next-step tool.

CRM: Alloy sweep around metrics; Flow currents at the lead chart; Inspection grid at the field desk; Case halo around guided intake.

All twelve use shared lightweight rendering with different placement, composition and pacing. Pointer light follows locally without recording or transmitting pointer data. Hidden/offscreen effects pause; reduced-motion and Motion off remain supported.

Three guides: customer symptom/scope/contact flow; owner workflow/evidence/responsibility guide; CRM observation/history/priority checklist. Red accents identify selected choices, progress and primary next actions. Results are guidance, not proof that a workflow is operational.

## Best next additions

1. A before/after viewer using approved real inspection footage, with captions and a keyboard-operable comparison control. More useful proof than adding another decorative film.
2. Once the intake connection is verified, carry the service-finder's allowed service choice into the quote form, avoiding repeated entry. Do not treat guide completion as a submitted lead.
3. Measure completed inquiries and mobile loading responsiveness before adding a heavier real-time 3D inspection scene. Analytics and live business integration need separate verified setup.
4. Develop a single optional interactive pipe-inspection chapter with Inspect / Clean / Verify states, preserving direct navigation and the existing reduced-motion fallback.

Avoid full-page flashes, scroll hijacking, mandatory sound, fabricated dashboard numbers and decorative effects over form fields. These would make the product harder to use without adding trustworthy business value.
