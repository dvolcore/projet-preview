# Owner decisions needed to activate

Answer these in the private handoff or conversation; do not paste passwords, access tokens or customer data.

1. Existing CRM/field-service platform, integration agent/task name and operator responsible for approving its handoff.
2. Final domain, current host/DNS owner, who can redirect the legacy domain, intended legal/trading business name and public facts. Approve one price range: the reviewed sources conflicted between $350–600 and $450–800 for hydro jetting. Do not replace either with a guessed value.
3. Current phone/SMS, calendar/dispatch, payment/accounting, footage storage and customer portal providers, account owners and available plan capabilities.
4. Crew count, service zones/hours, equipment, duration/travel buffers, emergency capacity, after-hours responder and backup. Who can promise same-day service or approve exceptions?
5. Approved price book, fees/taxes, estimate/repair exclusions, warranty promises, refund policy and financial approval limits.
6. Google Business Profile/Search Console/Bing/analytics ownership; website facts, privacy notices, contact consent and recording policies; responsible professional/provider review when required.
7. Monthly software/spend ceiling, owner dashboard recipients and AI autonomy limits. Who approves final diagnosis, reports, binding quotes, customer communications and emergency escalation?
8. Controlled test recipients, sandbox access procedure, pilot window, human fallback, rollback owner and exact production activation approval.

Research is needed against the selected provider's current official documentation: plan-specific APIs and limits, signed webhook behavior, native booking/capacity support, SMS registration/opt-out, export/backup, portal permissions, retention, payment reconciliation and incident history. Do this after platform identification, before purchasing or writing a custom substitute.

Business blind spots: unassigned leads, unstaffed emergency promises, unavailable crews, inconsistent old prices, duplicate indexed pages, consent drift, repeated follow-ups, payment authorization confused with settlement, private footage exposed through sharing, demand beyond capacity, vendor lock-in and failed restores. Growth is constrained by verified fulfillment, not visual polish.

Human authority remains necessary for field work, diagnosis, binding commercial commitments, disputed payments, exceptional refunds and serious incidents. AI may draft and route approved workflows; it does not independently own these decisions.
