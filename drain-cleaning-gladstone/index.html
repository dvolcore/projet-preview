Gladstone housing and what it means for your pipe

Built 1950 to 1979, with almost nothing before or since.

Gladstone barely existed before World War Two. The area was called Linden, and residents voted in November 1952 to incorporate, largely to avoid annexation by Kansas City. It had 14,502 people by 1960 and 23,422 by 1970. Census American Community Survey estimates put the median year built at 1973, with only about 3 percent of housing predating 1950 and roughly 62 percent built between 1950 and 1979.

That is one of the most concentrated housing profiles in the metro, and it produces one of the most predictable drain profiles. Cast iron drain, waste and vent inside the house, a lateral of clay or early plastic out to the street, and five to seven decades of scale and root intrusion. The symptom is almost always the same: a line that has been gradually slowing for years and now clogs on ordinary use.

The city's position reinforces it. Gladstone has no treatment plant of its own and sends all of its sanitary sewage to Kansas City for treatment, and its sewer collection division cleans and flushes on the order of 150,000 linear feet of sanitary sewer a year while rehabilitating and sealing lines to cut inflow and infiltration. Public mains getting that kind of attention sit alongside private laterals that get none until they back up.

Because Gladstone pays Kansas City to treat its flow, the city has a direct financial reason to keep groundwater and rainwater out of its sewers, and it has funded line sealing specifically to do that. Homeowners carry the same logic on a smaller scale. An uncapped cleanout, a downspout tied into a drain, or a cracked lateral all put water where it does not belong, and a camera inspection is what finds it.

Questions

Drain cleaning in Gladstone, answered

Why do so many Gladstone houses have the same drain problem?
Because they were built at the same time. About 62 percent of Gladstone housing went up between 1950 and 1979, so a whole city's worth of laterals reached the same age and the same condition at once.
Where does Gladstone sewage actually go?
The city states it has no treatment plant and sends all of its sanitary sewage to Kansas City for treatment. The collection mains are the city's. The lateral from your house to the main is yours.
Will chemical drain cleaner fix a recurring clog?
No. It can damage pipe and it rarely removes the actual blockage, which on a 1960s Gladstone line is usually roots or a scaled wall. We take the clog out and show you what it was.
