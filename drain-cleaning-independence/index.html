Independence housing and what it means for your pipe

Cast iron, clay tile, and fifty years of tree roots.

Independence is where the metro's drain problems are oldest and most honest. Census American Community Survey estimates put the median year built at 1967, with about 20 percent of all housing predating 1950 and a full half of it built between 1950 and 1979. In pipe terms that is a clay tile and cast-iron core wrapped in a thick 1950s-to-1970s band of cast iron.

Cast iron fails from the inside out. Scale builds on the pipe wall, the effective diameter shrinks year by year, and the drain that used to run fine now gurgles. Clay tile fails at the joints, which is where roots get in. Independence's own sewer maintenance crews list root sawing, smoke testing and dye testing among their standard work on a system of more than 578 miles of sanitary sewer and over 12,000 manholes, which tells you how common both failure modes are here.

One correction worth making, because plumbers get it wrong: Independence does not have combined sewers. The city states plainly that it runs separate sanitary and storm systems. Its federal sewer action, filed in 2009, was about sanitary sewer overflows, not stormwater mixing. So if your basement floor drain is backing up in a hard rain, the cause is on your side or in your lateral, not the storm system, and it is findable.

Where we work in Independence

The full 64050 through 64058 spread. The pre-war stock along Truman Road, US 24 and the Fairmount and Mount Washington corridors, the postwar build-out around South Noland Road and 23rd Street, and the newer east-side neighborhoods out toward Route 291 and I-470. Those are three different pipe eras inside one city limit and they do not get the same approach.

Tree roots are the other Independence constant. Kansas City's own urban forest inventory found that the metro's highest canopy sits over neighborhoods built between 1950 and 1980, and that maples make up nearly a quarter of street trees. University of Missouri Extension singles out silver maple as notorious for plugging sewer lines, and sweetgum, sycamore, ash and the larger maples are all documented aggressive rooters. Independence has all of them, planted over clay tile.

Questions

Drain cleaning in Independence, answered

Does Independence have combined storm and sanitary sewers?
No. The city states it operates separate sanitary and storm sewer systems. If your drain backs up when it rains, the inflow is getting in somewhere closer to home and a camera inspection can find it.
My house is from the 1920s. Is the pipe clay?
Often, yes, with cast iron inside the house. About one in five Independence homes predates 1950. A camera run confirms the material and the condition before anyone talks about digging.
Which trees cause the most sewer problems in Independence?
Silver maple is the one Missouri Extension names outright for plugging sewer lines. Sweetgum, sycamore, ash and the larger maples are also documented aggressive rooters, and older Independence streets are full of them.
