Kansas City housing and what it means for your pipe

The oldest pipe in the metro, and a combined sewer under part of it.

Kansas City's housing is genuinely old. Census American Community Survey estimates put the citywide median year built at 1969, but that number hides the core: more than 21 percent of all Kansas City housing units predate 1940 and roughly 27 percent predate 1950. The citywide figure is dragged forward by Northland growth. In the older neighborhoods the real number is far earlier, and so is the pipe.

Kansas City Water runs roughly 2,800 miles of sewer, and the city says plainly that parts of the system are 50 to 150 years old. About 56 to 58 square miles south of the Missouri River is a combined sewer, where sanitary and storm flow share one pipe, which is the entire reason the city is under a federal consent decree entered in 2010 and now running to 2040 under its Smart Sewer program. A combined system means heavy rain raises the level in the main your lateral ties into.

That is directly relevant to your house. In a combined sewer area, a basement floor drain or a basement toilet can back up during a storm even when nothing is wrong inside, and a partially blocked lateral that coped fine in dry weather will not cope then. The way to know which you have is to put a camera in the line and look, instead of snaking it every spring.

Where we work in Kansas City

The east side and the core first, because that is where we are closest and where the pipe is oldest. Independence Avenue and the Northeast, Troost and Prospect, Hyde Park and Midtown, Westport and the Plaza, Brookside and the Country Club district, and out along I-70, I-435 and Blue Parkway.

Street trees are a bigger factor here than anywhere else we work. Kansas City's own 2018 urban forest master plan found that maples are 23 percent of the street tree population, with sweetgum at 7 percent and sycamore at 3 percent. University of Missouri Extension names silver maple as notorious for plugging sewer lines outright, and sweetgum and sycamore are both documented aggressive rooters. Put those over 100-year-old clay tile and root intrusion stops being a surprise.

Drain cleaning in Kansas City, answered

Why does my basement drain back up only when it rains?
In Kansas City's combined sewer area, sanitary and storm flow share one pipe, so heavy rain raises the level in the main. A camera inspection shows whether your lateral is also restricted, which is the part you can actually fix.
How old is the sewer under an older Kansas City neighborhood?
The city states parts of its system are 50 to 150 years old, and more than a fifth of Kansas City housing predates 1940. In the core neighborhoods, clay tile and cast iron are the normal finding.
Do you clear drains for restaurants and commercial buildings?
Yes. Restaurants, businesses and industrial facilities, including grease-loaded kitchen lines and floor drains, with hydro jetting where a cable will not restore the pipe.
