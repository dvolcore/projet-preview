North Kansas City housing and what it means for your pipe

A 1912 industrial city, and the pipe shows it.

North Kansas City is a planned early twentieth century city. The north bank levee was built between 1909 and 1912 to make the river bottoms developable, and the city incorporated on 4 November 1912. Its population peaked at 5,657 in 1960, declined for four decades, and has been climbing again since 2000.

That history is written into the housing. Census American Community Survey estimates show about 33 percent of North Kansas City housing units predating 1950, the highest share of any city we serve, with just 1.5 percent built in the 1990s and roughly 20 percent built in the 2000s. It is a barbell, not a bell curve: a pre-war core plus recent infill, with almost nothing in between.

For drains that means two completely separate conversations. In the pre-war stock we expect clay tile laterals, cast iron inside, decades of scale, root intrusion at the joints, and in some cases pipe that has simply reached the end of its service life. In the 2000s infill we expect plastic and mechanical problems. A camera run is not a nice extra here, it is the only way to know which building you are standing in.

Clay County service area

Where we work in North Kansas City

64116 and 64117. Along Armour Road, which is the city's spine, up and down Burlington Street on Route 9, the Chouteau Trafficway on Route 269, and the I-29, I-35 and US 71 corridors that run through the city on their way across the river.

One thing worth knowing about a levee-protected bottomland city: stormwater here has to be pumped rather than simply flowing downhill, and the city describes its stormwater network as inlet drains, underground conveyance pipes and pumping stations. That is a separate system from the sanitary line your fixtures drain into. If water is coming up a floor drain, we start at the sanitary lateral, because that is both the most likely cause and the part you control.

Often, yes. About a third of the city's housing predates 1950, so clay tile laterals and cast iron inside the building are common findings. A camera inspection shows the material and the condition before anything is quoted.
Do you work on commercial buildings in NKC?
Yes. North Kansas City has a lot of older commercial and light industrial stock, and we clear restaurant kitchen lines, floor drains and main lines, with hydro jetting where a cable cannot restore the pipe.
