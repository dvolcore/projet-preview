Oak Grove housing and what it means for your pipe

Half 1970s, half brand new, and the city documents both.

Oak Grove is split almost evenly down the middle. Census American Community Survey estimates put the median year built at 1987, but the distribution is what matters: about 37 percent of housing went up between 1950 and 1979, with the 1970s alone accounting for 23 percent, and another 32 percent has been built since 2000 after the city grew by nearly 41 percent in a single decade.

The city's own adopted comprehensive plan spells out the consequence. Oak Grove maintains 252,218 linear feet of gravity sewer main, eight to thirty inches, made of vitreous clay pipe and PVC, with 1,046 manholes. That is not a guess about your pipe material, it is the published inventory, and it means the right answer depends entirely on which half of town you are in.

On the clay side, off Broadway and through the older grid, we are looking for roots at joints and a pipe wall that has been collecting grease for forty years. On the PVC side, out in the subdivisions that broke ground after 2000, we are looking for bellied line, settled backfill and root hair at gaskets. The symptom is a slow drain either way. The fix is not the same, and the camera is what decides it.

Is my Oak Grove sewer line clay or PVC?
Either, depending on where you are. The city's published inventory lists its 252,218 feet of gravity main as vitreous clay pipe and PVC. A camera run on your lateral settles it in minutes.
Who is responsible for the ditch and driveway pipe at my property?
The city states roadside ditches and driveway pipes are the property owner's responsibility, as are detention basins held by homeowner associations and private businesses. We clear what is in them.
