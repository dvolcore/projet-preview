Olathe housing and what it means for your pipe

A new city, which means new failure modes.

Olathe's growth is recent and relentless. It went from under 18,000 people in 1970 to over 141,000 in 2020, and Census American Community Survey estimates put the median year built at 1994. Roughly 23 percent of the housing stock was built in the 2000s alone, only about a quarter predates 1980, and under three percent predates 1950.

A city that new has very little cast iron in the ground, so the slow annual narrowing that defines drain work in Raytown or Independence is uncommon here. What we find in Olathe subdivisions is a line that bellied where the trench settled, a long lateral with marginal fall where grease from the kitchen stops at the first low spot, and root hair working in at a gasketed joint once the landscaping matured. All three present as a slow drain. Only one of them is fixed by a cable.

The exception is the original downtown around Santa Fe Street and the courthouse square, where the pre-1950 housing is concentrated. That is a genuinely old pocket inside a new city, and on those blocks we are back to clay tile joints and root cutting.

Questions

Drain cleaning in Olathe, answered

My Olathe house is from 2003. Why is the main line slow?
Most often a belly in the line where backfill settled, not enough fall over a long run, or root hair at a pipe joint. A camera inspection distinguishes them, which matters because only one of the three is solved by snaking.
Does a cable damage PVC pipe?
Used properly, no, but a cable in a bellied or low-slope line only buys you time, because the standing water and the solids it holds are still there. That is why we camera the line rather than guess.
Can you handle a grease-loaded restaurant line?
Yes. Hydro jetting is the right tool for grease because it strips the pipe wall back to full diameter instead of cutting a channel through the deposit. We also run the camera after so you can see the result.
