Pleasant Hill housing and what it means for your pipe

An 1865 railroad core inside a post-1990 city.

Pleasant Hill was platted in 1844, and in 1865 the town center physically moved about a mile southwest to the Pacific Railroad railhead. It was larger in 1870 than it was in 1960. Then it more than doubled between 1990 and 2010. Census American Community Survey estimates put the median year built at 1993, but about 13 percent of the housing stock still predates 1950.

The downtown historic district carries 53 contributing buildings developed between roughly 1865 and 1959, including the 1903 Missouri Pacific depot. On those blocks and in Old Town, the sewer work is old-pipe work: clay tile laterals, cast iron inside, scale, root intrusion at the joints, and in places pipe that has outlived its design life. Out in Sugarland, Hickory Hills and the newer additions, it is plastic and the problems are mechanical.

This is a city where guessing is expensive. A cable run blind down a 1900s lateral can find a joint that has already dropped, and a cable run blind down a 2005 lateral tells you nothing about the belly that is actually holding the water. Camera first, then the right tool.

Where we work in Pleasant Hill

64080, plus the 64034 side of the city that sits in Jackson County. Along Route 7, which is the main north-south route through town, out Route 58 on West Commercial Street, along the Route 150 outer belt, and through the First Street and Wyoming Street downtown grid.

The regional treatment plant sits inside Pleasant Hill, on East Route 58, but it is owned by the Little Blue Valley Sewer District's Middle Big Creek subdistrict rather than the city, and Pleasant Hill pays wholesale treatment costs per connection. The city oversees the collection system. The lateral from your building to the main is yours, and on the older side of town that is the single most likely place for a backup to begin.

Drain cleaning in Pleasant Hill, answered

How old is the pipe in downtown Pleasant Hill?
The downtown historic district's buildings date from about 1865 to 1959, and roughly 13 percent of city housing predates 1950. In that core, clay tile laterals and cast iron inside the building are the normal finding.
I am out in a newer subdivision. Same problems?
Different ones. Post-1990 Pleasant Hill construction is plastic, so instead of scale and failed clay we look for bellied line, marginal fall and root hair at gasketed joints. The camera tells them apart.
Do you charge extra to come out to Pleasant Hill?
We quote the job, not the drive. Hydro jetting is $450 to $800 and snaking is $150 to $300 depending on line size, access, run length and what is in the pipe, and you get the number before we start.
