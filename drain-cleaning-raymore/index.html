Raymore housing and what it means for your pipe

A PVC town, which rules a lot of causes out.

Raymore was a village of under 600 people as late as 1970. It has grown every decade since, and Census American Community Survey estimates put the median year built at 2002, the newest of any city on our map. Roughly 78 percent of its housing has been built since 1990, under 14 percent predates 1980, and only about 127 housing units in the entire city predate 1960.

That is a genuinely useful diagnostic. In Raymore we can set aside the scaled cast iron and failed clay tile that dominate work in Raytown or Independence, because the pipe is almost all plastic. What is left are the mechanical failures: a lateral that bellied where the trench backfill settled, a long run with marginal fall where grease stalls at the first low spot, and root hair finding its way in at a gasketed joint now that the subdivision trees have matured.

Those three causes look identical at the fixture and respond very differently to treatment. A cable through a bellied line restores flow for a while and changes nothing about the sag. Jetting clears grease and root hair properly. Neither fixes a slope problem. This is why the camera comes first here rather than last.

Questions

Drain cleaning in Raymore, answered

My Raymore house is practically new. Why do I have a main line clog?
Because new pipe still settles. In a post-1990 city the usual causes are a belly from settled backfill, not enough fall on a long run, or root hair at a joint. None of those are about the age of the material.
Should I just have the line snaked every year?
If a line needs annual snaking, something in it has not been fixed. A camera inspection shows whether the cause is grease, roots or a sag, and only one of those three is actually solved by a cable.
