Basement Floor Drain Backing Up: What It Means

A basement floor drain almost never clogs on its own. It is the lowest opening in the building's drainage system, so when something downstream blocks, that drain is where the water comes out. The floor drain is the symptom.

Why it is the floor drain

Waste water leaves your house by gravity. When the path downstream is blocked, the water in the pipe rises until it finds an opening. The lowest opening in the system is the basement floor drain, so that is where it appears. Plunging it or pouring something into it is working on the one part of the system that is functioning correctly. The real question is always what is downstream.

Cause 1: the building drain or sewer lateral is blocked

This is the most common cause. Roots, grease, sediment in a sagged section, wipes, scale, or a pipe that has actually failed.

How to tell: the backup gets worse when water is used upstairs. Flush a toilet or start the washer and the level at the floor drain rises. Other fixtures in the house are also slow. Stop using water immediately, because every gallon goes onto the floor. See main line clog vs single drain clog.

Cause 2: the storm side

In many houses the floor drain is tied to a sump, a footing drain, or a combined sewer. Then the backup is a water-volume problem rather than a blockage.

How to tell: it happens during or shortly after heavy rain, the water is relatively clean, and the sump pump is either running constantly or not running at all. Check for a failed pump, a stuck float, a tripped breaker, or a discharge line that is frozen, crushed or blocked.

Cause 3: the city main is surcharged

The municipal sewer is temporarily full, usually in a heavy rain event, and your house is draining into a pipe with nowhere to go.

How to tell: neighbours have it at the same time, and it stops when the rain does. Nothing in your line is wrong. For houses with a history of this, a backwater valve on the building drain is the device that addresses reverse flow, and it is worth raising with your plumber. Clearing a line does not help a surcharge.

What to do in the first ten minutes

  • Stop all water use. Shut off the washer and the dishwasher mid-cycle. Every gallon raises the level.
  • Do not pour chemical cleaner into standing sewage water. It will not reach the blockage and it makes the cleanup hazardous.
  • Treat the water as contaminated. Gloves and boots. Keep children and pets out of the area.
  • Move what you can off the floor.
  • Note whether it is raining and whether the sump is running. That single observation tells us which of the three causes we are walking into.
  • Call. We answer 24 hours a day, every day of the year, holidays included.

What we do, and what happens after

We locate the cleanout, clear the line, and then camera it. The camera is not an upsell, it is the part that tells you whether this happens again. A floor drain that has backed up once will back up again if the cause was a sag, an offset joint or roots at a separated joint, because cleaning removes the debris and leaves the defect. If the camera shows a defect, that is sewer line repair. If it shows sound pipe, you need an interval, not a repair. Rates for drain cleaning and jetting are on pricing.

Questions

Floor Drain Backing Up FAQ

Should I plunge the basement floor drain?
No. The blockage is not in the floor drain, it is downstream of it. Plunging a floor drain achieves nothing and splashes contaminated water.
Is the water coming up the floor drain dangerous?
Treat it as contaminated. Wear gloves and boots, keep children and pets away, and do not run equipment through standing water.
Why does my floor drain only back up when it rains?
That pattern points away from a blockage and toward the storm side: a sump or footing drain problem, or a city main that is temporarily full. The fix is different from clearing a line.
Will cleaning the line stop it happening again?
Only if the pipe behind the clog is sound. That is why the line is cameraed after it is cleared. A sag, an offset joint or an open joint with roots will fill in again.
