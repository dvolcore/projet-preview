Brown Friday: Why the Day After Thanksgiving Is the Biggest Drain Day of the Year

Plumbers call the day after Thanksgiving Brown Friday. It is not a joke about the name. It is the one day of the year when three separate loads land on the same pipe inside twenty-four hours, and the lines that were already half full of grease or roots are the ones that give up.

What the day actually looks like

Roto-Rooter, which publishes its own national call figures, reports a 50 percent spike in service calls on the day after Thanksgiving, and about a 21 percent increase across the Thursday-through-Sunday holiday weekend. Those are one company's internal numbers rather than independent research, so treat them as an order of magnitude rather than a measurement. They match what drain crews see: the phone does not ring any differently on Thanksgiving morning, and then it does not stop on Friday.

The reason the peak lands on Friday and not Thursday is worth understanding, because it tells you what is actually happening inside the pipe.

Three loads, one pipe, twenty-four hours

Load one is fat. Turkey drippings, gravy, roasting pan residue, butter and bacon fat go into the sink as a hot liquid on Thursday afternoon. They do not stay liquid. Animal fats are solid at room temperature, so they only have to stop being hot to set, and the inside of a buried drain line in late November in Kansas City is a long way from room temperature. The fat deposits on the pipe wall within the first few feet of horizontal run and keeps depositing all the way down.

Load two is solids. Potato peels, stuffing, rice, pasta and vegetable trimmings go in at the same time, usually through a disposal. Starch absorbs water and turns to paste. Long plant fibres do not shorten enough in a disposal to stop behaving like fibres. Both embed in the fat layer and make it denser and tougher than either material would be alone.

Load three is water volume. This is the one most people miss, and it is the one that arrives on Friday. A house that normally runs two showers and one dishwasher cycle a day runs eight showers, three dishwasher loads and the bedding from every guest room. Drain lines are sized for a peak flow on the assumption that fixtures do not all discharge at once. With a full house, they do.

Why the failure is on Friday

A drain line almost never fails at the moment of the insult. It fails at the next peak-flow event after the insult. Thursday's grease needs several hours in a cold pipe to cool, consolidate and bond to the wall. By Friday morning the restriction is in place and the house starts running every fixture it owns. Water arriving faster than the narrowed section can pass it backs up behind the restriction, velocity drops in the backed-up length, the solids that were in suspension settle out, and what was a slow drain at breakfast is a backup by lunchtime.

This is also why the warning signs show up days earlier. If a sink is draining slower this week than it did last month, the line already has less diameter than it should and the holiday is simply the load test.

The lines that fail are the ones that were already restricted

A sewer line in good condition handles a Thanksgiving weekend without complaint. The ones that fail have a pre-existing condition, and it is almost always one of three:

  • Grease and scale on the wall. Years of kitchen waste have narrowed the bore. Read why a grease clog comes back for what that deposit is made of.
  • Root intrusion. Fine roots through a joint act like a strainer. Everything fibrous that comes down the line catches on them. See tree roots in a sewer line.
  • Lost slope or a belly. A section that has settled holds standing water, so solids drop out there every single day of the year. Why a drain is slow covers how that gets misread as a clog.

If every fixture in the house slowed down at the same time, the problem is the main line rather than one branch. The main line clog versus single drain clog test takes two minutes and tells you which conversation you are having.

The window you have

The useful work happens in the two to three weeks before the holiday, not during it. A camera inspection shows what is actually in the line and where, so the fix is chosen rather than guessed at. If the camera shows a wall deposit, hydro jetting cuts it off the wall and restores full diameter rather than punching a channel through it. If it shows a broken or collapsed section, cleaning will not help and sewer line repair or full replacement is the honest answer. Questions

Brown Friday FAQ

Why is it called Brown Friday?
It is the plumbing trade's name for the day after Thanksgiving, which is the heaviest call day of the year for drain and sewer work. Roto-Rooter reports roughly a 50 percent spike in service calls that day, based on its own company figures.
Why does the drain fail on Friday instead of Thursday?
Grease poured on Thursday needs hours in a cold pipe to cool and bond to the wall. The restriction is in place by Friday morning, which is when the house runs showers, dishwashers and laundry all at once and load-tests the narrowed line.
My drain is a little slow this week. Will it get through Thanksgiving?
A slow drain means the line has already lost diameter. It is far cheaper to have it cleared on a scheduled visit now than during a holiday backup with a full house.
