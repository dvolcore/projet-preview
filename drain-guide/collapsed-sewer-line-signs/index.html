Clog, break, collapse

These are three different conditions and they are often discussed as one.

  • A clog is a blocked path through intact pipe. Remove the material and the line works.
  • A break or crack is damaged pipe that is still roughly round. It leaks, admits roots and soil, and catches solids.
  • A collapse is loss of structure. The barrel has broken and lost its shape, or a section has dropped out entirely and soil has moved in.

The distinction matters financially. Clearing equipment can push past the debris that accumulates at a collapse and give you a few days of normal drainage. The drain works, the invoice is paid, and three weeks later it blocks again. That cycle is the single most common reason a homeowner pays for drain cleaning repeatedly on a line that needed repair.

The signs

  • Repeat backups on a shortening interval. Months, then weeks, then days.
  • The line clears but the camera cannot pass. The cable stops at the same footage every visit. That is the strongest single indicator.
  • A soft or sunken spot in the yard, lawn or driveway along the line's path. Soil migrates into the broken pipe and the ground above loses support. A depression over a sewer line is a serious sign.
  • A stripe of unusually lush, fast-growing grass where waste water is feeding the soil.
  • Sewer odor outdoors near the line, or in a crawl space.
  • Persistent damp or new cracking near the line's path indoors. Soil washing out from under a slab can cause settlement.
  • Rodents or drain flies appearing indoors. Both travel sewer lines and enter through breaks.
  • Every fixture slow at once and it does not resolve after cleaning. See main line vs single drain.

Why lines collapse

Age and material account for most of it. Clay tile cracks and shears at the joints. Orangeburg, a bituminous fibre pipe, deforms into an oval and then flattens. Cast iron corrodes through at the bottom of the pipe. Add root wedging, ground movement, loss of bedding under the pipe, heavy loads over a shallow run, and prior excavation nearby, and you have the full list.

How it gets confirmed

With a camera and a locator, not with a guess. The camera shows the break and the footage at which it occurs. The locator picks up the transmitter in the camera head from grade and gives the exact surface position and depth. That is what turns a repair into one targeted excavation instead of a trench across the yard. Before you approve any dig, ask to see the footage and the marked location.

If it is backing up right now

Stop all water use, because every gallon you send down goes either into the house or into the soil. Do not pour chemical cleaner into a line that is going to be excavated. Keep people and pets away from any standing waste water. Then call. We answer 24 hours a day, every day of the year, including Thanksgiving, Christmas Eve and Christmas Day. Questions

Collapsed Sewer Line FAQ

Can a collapsed sewer line be snaked?
Not as a fix. Equipment can push through the debris that piles up at the collapse, which restores drainage briefly, but the pipe has lost its structure and it will block again.
How do you know where to dig?
The camera head carries a transmitter. A locator picks it up from the surface at the defect and gives the exact position and depth, so the excavation is made at one marked point.
Will my whole yard be dug up?
No. Locating the defect first means only the damaged run is excavated. Full-length replacement is only recommended when the camera shows defects along the whole line.
Is a collapsed sewer line an emergency?
If waste water is entering the house, yes. If drainage is still working, it is urgent rather than an emergency, but it will not improve on its own and the soil loss around the break continues.
