A Drain Backs Up Mid-Meal: What to Check Before You Call

This page is for the ten minutes between noticing and dialling. It will not clear your line. It will tell you whether you are dealing with one fixture or the whole house, and it will stop you doing the three things that reliably make a holiday backup worse.

First, stop adding water

Everything that runs in the house goes to the same line and comes back up at the lowest opening. Shut off taps, stop the dishwasher mid-cycle, stop the washing machine, and tell the other fourteen people in the house to stop flushing. One guest flushing upstairs undoes everything you are about to do. The full pre-arrival checklist, including when to shut the water off at the main valve, is on the emergency drain service page.

Then find out how big the problem is

This is the part worth doing, because the answer changes what happens next and it takes about two minutes.

  1. Check a fixture on the other side of the house. Run a bathroom sink far from the kitchen. If it drains normally, the problem is a branch line and the rest of the house is fine. If it is slow too, the problem is downstream of both and you are looking at the main.
  2. Watch the lowest opening in the house. Usually a basement floor drain, otherwise a ground-floor shower. Have someone flush a toilet while you stand there. Water appearing at the floor drain when a toilet is flushed means the main line is blocked and every fixture in the house is now a liability. The reasoning is in main line clog versus single drain clog.
  3. Listen for gurgling. If a tub or toilet gurgles when something else drains, air is being drawn through a water seal because the pipe below is restricted. Gurgling at a fixture that is not the one you are using is a main line symptom, not a local one.

Kitchen-specific checks

  • Double bowl sink. If water rises in the second bowl when you run the first, the blockage is past the tee where the two bowls join, which puts it in the branch line rather than under the basket.
  • Dishwasher backing up into the sink. The dishwasher drains into the same branch. If dirty water appears in the sink during a cycle, stop the cycle; continuing pumps more water into a line that cannot take it.
  • Disposal humming but not turning. That is a jam, not a clog, and it is fixable in five minutes. Switch it off, then switch off the breaker to it. Fit the hex key that came with the unit into the socket in the centre of the underside and work it back and forth until it turns freely. Remove whatever comes loose, press the red reset button on the underside, restore power and run cold water. A disposal that is silent and dead is usually just the reset button.
  • Disposal running normally but water will not go down. That is downstream. The disposal is not the problem and running it more will not help.

Three things that make it worse

  • Chemical drain cleaner. It rarely clears a deposit bonded to the pipe wall, it can damage pipe and fixtures, and it leaves a caustic liquid standing in the line for whoever opens it next. Pro Jet does not use harsh chemicals. If you have already poured some in, say so on the phone and do not plunge it afterwards — plunging a sink full of caustic solution sprays it.
  • Opening a cleanout with a full house upstairs. A cleanout cap on a blocked main can be holding back the contents of every pipe above it. If you do not know how much is backed up behind it, leave it and let someone with a wet vacuum and the right equipment open it.
  • Boiling water down a grease line. It melts the surface of the deposit and carries it further down to set again somewhere less accessible. Where cooking grease actually goes covers why.

What is worth doing while you wait

Clear absorbent items out of the water's path, lift what you can off the floor, and keep everyone back from the standing water, which is contaminated. An outlet, furnace or water heater anywhere near the spread is a reason to leave the room entirely and mention it when you phone. Photograph or film it before anything gets cleaned up, because that record is worth having for both the diagnosis and any claim.

Call now or wait until Monday?

Call now if any drain in the house is backing up, if water is appearing at a floor drain, if the toilets will not clear, or if there is sewage odor through the house. A blocked main does not clear itself and every fixture in the building feeds it. Call in the morning if exactly one fixture is slow and the rest of the house is normal, because that is a branch line problem and it will wait a few hours. Basement floor drain backing up explains why the floor drain is a symptom rather than the fault.

Questions

Backing Up Mid-Meal FAQ

How do I know if it is one drain or the whole house?
Run a fixture far from the one that is backing up. If it drains normally the problem is a branch line. If it is slow too, or water appears at a basement floor drain when a toilet is flushed, the main line is blocked.
My garbage disposal is humming but not turning. What do I do?
That is a jam rather than a clog. Switch the disposal off, switch off its breaker, work the hex key that came with the unit in the socket under the centre of the housing until it turns freely, clear what comes loose, press the red reset button and restore power.
Should I use a chemical drain cleaner while I wait?
No. It rarely clears a deposit bonded to the pipe wall, it can damage pipe and fixtures, and it leaves caustic liquid standing in the line for the next person to open it. Never plunge a fixture that has had chemical cleaner poured into it.
Is it safe to open the sewer cleanout myself?
Not during a backup with a full house. The cap can be holding back everything in the pipes above it, and there is no way to judge how much from the outside.
