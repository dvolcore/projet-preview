Why winter is a different problem

Cold changes the mechanics of a drain line in four ways that matter, and they compound with the holiday cooking load rather than just sitting alongside it.

Buried pipe is cold. Ground temperature at the depth a sewer lateral is buried follows the seasonal air temperature with a lag of weeks to months, which means the coldest ground of the year arrives after the coldest air. Fats, oils and grease set faster and harder in a December lateral than in a July one. The same cup of turkey fat that would have travelled thirty feet in summer sets within a much shorter run, and the deposit it leaves is thicker.

Soil moves. The clay soils common across eastern Jackson County shrink, swell and heave with freeze-thaw cycles. That movement works on the weakest part of an old line, which is the joint. In clay tile sewer pipe the joints are where roots get in and where offsets develop, and winter is when a marginal joint becomes a separated one.

Root masses stay put even though roots stop growing. Tree root growth slows in winter, which leads people to assume a root problem is seasonal. The mass already inside the pipe does not go anywhere. It keeps working as a strainer on everything fibrous that comes down the line, and holiday waste is the most fibrous material it will catch all year. See tree roots in a sewer line.

Empty houses dry their traps. Every drain has a water seal in its trap holding sewer gas out of the building. Water evaporates. A house left empty for a week over the holidays, with the heat running, can lose the seal in a guest bathroom or a floor drain and smell strongly of sewer gas when the family gets back. The fix is often nothing more than running water into the fixture, which is worth knowing before anyone calls a plumber. Sewer smell in the house covers the other causes.

What the weather does to the repair, not just the blockage

Frozen ground and snow cover make excavation slower and more disruptive, which raises the value of getting the diagnosis right the first time. A camera inspection with a locator establishes what has failed, exactly where, and how deep, before anyone puts a shovel in frozen ground. Where the line is restricted but structurally sound, hydro jetting restores it to full diameter and buys the time to plan properly. Where the pipe has genuinely failed, sewer line repair and full replacement with excavation is the work, and knowing that in advance is the difference between one dig and two.

Are you really open on Christmas Day?

Do I get a real person or an answering service?
A real person. You are not leaving a message for someone to return after the holiday.
Why do drains fail more in winter?
Ground temperature at the depth a lateral is buried lags the air temperature by weeks, so buried pipe is at its coldest after the coldest weather. Fats set faster and harder in a cold line, and freeze-thaw movement in clay soil works on joints that were already marginal.
The house smelled of sewer gas after we were away. Why?
Trap seals evaporate. A fixture that has not been used for a week, especially a guest bathroom or a floor drain, can lose the water seal that holds sewer gas out. Running water into the fixture often restores it.
