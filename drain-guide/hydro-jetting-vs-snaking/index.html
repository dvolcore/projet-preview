Hydro Jetting vs Snaking: Which One Do You Need?

Snaking restores flow. Jetting restores diameter. Almost every real question about which one you need is answered by that sentence.

What each one physically does

Snaking feeds a steel cable down the line and rotates it. A cutting or retrieving head bores a channel through the blockage, or hooks it and pulls it back out. When it is done, water flows through a hole in the obstruction. The pipe wall is left coated with whatever was on it.

Hydro jetting feeds a hose with a reverse-facing nozzle down the line. High-pressure water, up to 4,000 PSI in our equipment, is aimed backward against the pipe wall. The jets shear material off the wall, the thrust pulls the hose upstream, and the water carries the debris back down to the cleanout or the main. When it is done, the pipe is clean to its original diameter.

When snaking is the right call

  • A single fixture with an obvious cause: hair in a bathroom sink, paper in a toilet, a toy in a trapway.
  • Retrieving an object rather than removing a deposit.
  • A line with no cleanout access and no history of repeat clogs.
  • A pipe the camera shows as fragile, where the plan is to restore flow gently and then repair the defect.

When jetting is the right call

  • Grease. Snaking a grease line punches a hole in it and the walls stay coated, so it closes again. Only jetting takes the deposit off the wall. See grease clogs.
  • Root mats. A cutter head leaves stubs at the wall; a root nozzle shears them back and flushes them out.
  • Scale in cast iron. Corrosion scale is bonded to the wall. Cable work knocks off the high spots and leaves the rest.
  • Sand, silt and sludge collected in a sagged section or a floor drain.
  • Any clog that has come back after snaking. The return is information: the deposit was never removed.
  • Restaurant and commercial lines on a maintenance interval. See commercial services.

What neither one fixes

Both are cleaning methods. Neither repairs pipe. A collapse, a cracked section, an offset joint or a belly is a repair, and cleaning a failed pipe just buys weeks. That is why a camera inspection belongs before the decision on any line that has clogged more than once. Jetting also needs an access point, a cleanout or an accessible opening, to get the hose in and the slurry out.

Questions

Hydro Jetting vs Snaking FAQ

Is hydro jetting safe for old pipes?
On sound pipe, yes, including old clay tile and cast iron. On pipe that is already cracked or corroded through, aggressive cleaning can open the defect, which is why the camera comes first and pressure is set to what the line shows.
Which is cheaper, snaking or hydro jetting?
Snaking costs less per visit, at $150 to $300 against $450 to $800 for hydro jetting. On a recurring clog, repeated snaking frequently costs more in total than jetting the line once.
Do I need a camera inspection first?
On a first-time clog at a single fixture, usually not. On a main line, or anything that has clogged before, yes. It is the only way to know whether you are cleaning a dirty pipe or a failing one.
Can hydro jetting cut through tree roots?
Yes. A root nozzle shears roots back at the pipe wall and restores full diameter. Severe root damage to the pipe itself still needs repair.
