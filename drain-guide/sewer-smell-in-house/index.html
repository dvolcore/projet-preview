Sewer Smell in the House: How to Find the Source

Your drains are connected to the sewer by design. What keeps the gas out is a set of water seals and a vent system. A smell indoors means one of those barriers has failed. Find the failed barrier and the smell stops.

The barriers that keep sewer gas out

  • Fixture traps. Every fixture holds a plug of water in a trap. The International Plumbing Code requires a trap seal of not less than two inches and not more than four inches of water. If that water leaves, the drain becomes an open pipe into your house.
  • The vent system. Vents let air into the drain so water flows without siphoning traps dry, and carry gas out above the roof.
  • Mechanical seals. The gasket between a toilet and its floor flange, cleanout plugs, a trap primer feeding a floor drain, and the washing machine standpipe.

Masking the smell does nothing, because the barrier is still open. Work through the list below instead.

Match the smell to its location

Basement, or a bathroom nobody uses

Almost always a dry trap. Floor drains, basement showers, laundry sinks and guest-bath fixtures lose their water seal to evaporation when nothing runs through them. Pour a gallon or two of water down each one and wait a day. A dry floor drain is the single most common source of a basement sewer smell. Where a floor drain is used rarely by design, a trap primer or an inline trap seal device keeps it wet.

Only when a particular fixture runs, or only at one fixture

A siphoned trap, a restricted vent serving that fixture, or a bad connection behind it. A partly blocked vent makes a fixture gurgle and pull its own trap seal down as it drains, which lets gas straight through between uses.

At a toilet, strongest near the floor

A failed seal at the closet flange, often with a toilet that rocks slightly when you sit on it. The gas is coming out around the base, not up through the bowl.

In the kitchen, worse right after running the disposer

Usually not sewer gas at all. Food film under the splash guard and inside the disposer chamber smells sour or rotten rather than like sewage. Clean it before suspecting the plumbing. A kitchen line that is also draining slowly is a grease issue.

Throughout the house, worse on windy days or when fans run

Exhaust fans, a clothes dryer and some furnaces put the house under slight negative pressure, which pulls gas through whatever weak point exists. The fans are not the problem; they are finding the problem for you.

Outdoors over the yard, or indoors with gurgling and slow drains

Now the barrier that failed is the pipe. A cracked or open joint in the lateral, an unplugged or missing cleanout cap, or a cracked stack inside a wall. This is the case that needs a camera inspection, and if the camera finds a break, repair.

Smell plus drain flies

Drain flies breed in organic film. They mean there is a persistently wet, dirty surface somewhere, which can be a neglected trap, a sagged section holding waste, or a break in a pipe. They are a useful clue rather than a separate problem.

Work from cheapest to most invasive

Fill every trap in the building, including floor drains and unused fixtures. Check the base of each toilet. Check for an open or missing cleanout plug, in the basement and outdoors. Run each fixture and smell at it. If the odour survives all of that, or if it comes with gurgling and slow drains, the failure is in the pipe and it needs to be looked at. Questions

Sewer Smell in House FAQ

Why does my basement smell like sewer?
Most often a dry floor drain trap. Water in the trap evaporates when nothing runs through it, and the drain becomes an open pipe to the sewer. Pour a gallon or two of water in and wait a day.
Is a sewer smell in the house dangerous?
Sewer gas is a mixture, and hydrogen sulfide in it is hazardous at high concentrations. A persistent strong odour indoors should be found and fixed rather than lived with.
Will bleach or an air freshener fix a sewer smell?
No. Both cover the odour while the open barrier stays open. The smell returns as soon as they wear off.
Why does the sewer smell come back every summer?
Evaporation. Traps in rarely used fixtures dry out faster in warm, dry conditions, so seasonal odour in a basement or spare bathroom usually means a trap that needs refilling or a trap primer.
