Why Is My Drain Slow? Eight Causes, Diagnosed

Slow is not the same as blocked. A slow drain still moves water, which means the cause is still diagnosable and the fix is still cheap. That changes once it stops.

1. A partial obstruction in the trap or stopper

Bathroom sinks and tubs collect hair bound with soap scum around the stopper rod and in the trap bend. It is the most common cause and the most local. One fixture affected, nothing else.

2. Grease coating a branch line

A kitchen line loses diameter evenly along its length rather than blocking at a point, so it gets slower over months. See grease clogs for why hot water and chemicals do not help.

3. Scale in cast iron

Corrosion products build nodular scale on the inside of old cast iron. It narrows the pipe and makes the wall rough, so paper and solids catch where they would otherwise pass. The line then clogs at a rate unrelated to what you put down it. See cast iron pipe failure.

4. A blocked or restricted vent

This is the cause most often missed. Drainage is not only gravity. Water running down a pipe has to be replaced by air, and that air comes through the vent system. Block the vent with a bird nest, leaves, ice or a remodel that capped a line, and the water has to pull air backward through a trap seal instead. The result is a fixture that glugs, drains slowly, and drains perfectly the moment someone loosens a cleanout. The tell is a slow drain with gurgling and sometimes odor, where cable work brings nothing back.

5. Lost slope: a belly or a flat run

Drain piping needs the right fall. The International Plumbing Code sets a minimum slope of a quarter inch per foot for pipe two and a half inches and smaller, and an eighth of an inch per foot for pipe three to six inches. Too flat and solids settle out. Too steep and the water outruns the solids and leaves them behind. A section that has settled below grade holds standing water permanently, and the camera shows water that does not drain away after the flow stops. That is a geometry problem, and cleaning does not fix geometry.

6. Roots or an offset joint in the lateral

A partial restriction out in the sewer line shows up as several fixtures running slow, worst at the lowest ones. See tree roots in a sewer line and main line vs single drain.

7. Something lodged in the pipe

A toothbrush, a bottle cap, a toy, a chunk of construction debris left in a remodel. Nothing passes it cleanly and everything catches on it, so the drain is permanently slow rather than progressively slower.

8. The line is restricted or surcharged downstream

A restricted shared line or city main, or a lateral that fills during heavy rain, makes everything slow at once. The giveaway is correlation with rainfall, or neighbours with the same symptom at the same time.

Diagnosing it in the right order

First work out which fixtures are affected. That tells you which segment of pipe is involved. Then work out which of four categories you are in: a deposit on the wall such as grease or scale, an obstruction such as hair, roots or an object, an air problem such as a blocked vent, or a geometry problem such as a sag. Each one calls for different work, and guessing wrong is how people pay twice. On anything in the main line or the lateral, a camera inspection settles it.

One thing not to do: pour chemical drain cleaner into a slow drain. A slow drain is still diagnosable, and a caustic liquid standing in a partly open pipe is a hazard for whoever opens it next. Our drain cleaning and hydro jetting pages cover the methods, with rates on pricing.

Questions

Slow Drain Causes FAQ

Is a slow drain urgent?
Not urgent, but worth acting on. A slow drain is the cheapest point to diagnose the problem. Once it stops completely, you lose the use of the fixture and often the choice of when the work happens.
Will a plunger fix a slow drain?
Sometimes, on a single fixture with a loose obstruction in the trap. A plunger does nothing for grease on the wall, scale, a blocked vent or a sagged pipe.
Why are all my drains running slow at once?
Because the restriction is downstream of all of them, in the building drain or the sewer lateral. That is a main line problem, not a fixture problem.
Can a blocked vent really cause a slow drain?
Yes, and it is routinely misdiagnosed as a clog. Without air coming in through the vent, water cannot flow freely and has to pull air back through a trap, which makes the drain gurgle and crawl.
