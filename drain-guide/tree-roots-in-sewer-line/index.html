Tree Roots in a Sewer Line: Signs, Causes and Fixes

Roots do not break into sound pipe. They find an opening that is already there, then thicken inside it. Understanding that sequence tells you whether your line needs cleaning or repair.

How roots get in

Roots grow toward water and nutrients. A sanitary sewer line is warm, wet and nutrient rich, and an older line leaks vapor at its joints. A hair-fine root tip finds a gap, a separated joint, a hairline crack or a badly cut branch connection, and grows through it. Inside the pipe it finds ideal conditions and branches into a dense mat.

Two things then happen at once. The mat catches toilet paper, grease and solids, so the pipe stops working as a pipe and starts working as a filter. And the root itself thickens, acting as a wedge that widens the opening it came through. That is why root intrusion gets worse on its own: the defect grows with the root.

Research backs this up. A 2001 review in the Journal of Infrastructure Systems, published by the American Society of Civil Engineers, found that roots cause more than half of all sewer blockages, and identified old jointed pipe, shallow or small-diameter pipe, and fast-growing tree species as the leading risk factors. That is a precise description of older Kansas City neighbourhoods: clay tile laid in short jointed sections, buried shallow, under mature street trees.

Signs, in the order they usually appear

  • A toilet gurgles when the washing machine drains or a tub empties.
  • More than one fixture slows down, with the lowest fixtures worst.
  • A toilet level rises, pauses, then drains slowly.
  • The basement floor drain or lowest shower backs up when water is used upstairs.
  • The same clog returns on a schedule. Roots grow seasonally, so spring and fall recurrences are a strong clue.
  • A stripe of unusually green, fast-growing grass, or a soft spot, over the line's path.

Which pipes roots get into

Older clay tile is the usual answer, because it is laid in short sections and has a joint every few feet. Orangeburg, early cast iron with packed joints, and any line with an unsealed saddle tap are also vulnerable. Modern gasketed or solvent-welded plastic rarely admits roots unless it has cracked, pulled apart or gone out of line.

Confirming it before spending anything

A camera inspection with a locator answers the only question that matters: are these roots in a pipe that is otherwise sound, or are they coming through a defect that has to be repaired? The camera shows the entry point and its condition. The locator marks the exact spot at grade and gives the depth, so if digging is needed it happens in one place rather than across the yard.

The real options

  • Mechanical cutting. A cable with a root-cutting head bores a channel through the mat. Fast and cheap. It leaves root stubs standing at the pipe wall, and regrowth is usually measured in months.
  • Hydro jetting. A root nozzle shears the roots back at the pipe wall and the water flushes the debris out, restoring full diameter instead of a channel. It buys considerably more time than cutting. Our hydro jetting runs up to 4,000 PSI.
  • Repair or replacement. When the camera shows the joint has separated, the pipe has cracked around the root, or a section has collapsed, cleaning is maintenance on a broken pipe. We do the excavation and replacement ourselves.
  • Copper sulfate and foaming root killers. These kill root tissue they contact. They do not remove the mat, and they do nothing about the opening the roots came through.

After it is cleared

If the opening was not repaired, the roots will come back, because the conditions that brought them have not changed. The sensible plan is an interval set from what the camera showed and how fast the regrowth was last time, rather than waiting for the next backup. Questions

Tree Roots in Sewer Line FAQ

Can roots be removed without digging?
Usually yes. Hydro jetting cuts roots back at the pipe wall and restores full diameter. Digging becomes necessary when the camera shows the pipe itself has cracked, separated or collapsed.
How fast do roots grow back?
It depends on how they were removed and whether the opening was repaired. Cutting a channel regrows fastest. Jetting back to the pipe wall buys longer. An unrepaired open joint will always re-root eventually.
Will removing the tree fix the problem?
No. The roots already in the pipe stay there, and the opening they entered through is still open. The defect in the pipe is what has to be addressed.
Does homeowners insurance cover a root-damaged sewer line?
Coverage for a buried sewer lateral is normally an add-on rather than part of a standard policy. Ask your carrier whether you have a service line endorsement before assuming you are covered.
