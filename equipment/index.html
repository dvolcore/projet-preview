The jetter

A trailer-mounted hydro jetter, rated up to 4,000 PSI.

It is trailer-mounted, which is the point. The unit is towed behind the truck, so the machine that arrives at a restaurant grease line is the same machine that arrives at a house, and it can be set where the cleanout is rather than where the street allows.

Pressure is not the whole story, but it is the part that decides what comes off the pipe wall. Up to 4,000 PSI of water does three different things to three different problems:

  • Grease. Congealed fat, oil and soap do not sit in the middle of a pipe, they line it. High-pressure water breaks that coating up and carries it out of the line instead of pushing a plug of it further downstream.
  • Scale. Hardened mineral and rust scale on the inside of old cast iron is brittle and bonded to the wall. Water at pressure shears it off, which is what descaling is, and the pipe gets its diameter back rather than its path back.
  • Roots. Small roots that have come through a joint get cut out. Heavy, established root intrusion, and the cracked or offset joint that let it in, is a repair and we will say so.

Pressure is sized to the line, not run flat out on everything. The reason the camera goes in first is that the line tells us what it needs; we do not decide before looking. Full detail on the cleaning itself is on the hydro jetting page, including the published range.

Why we publish the range

A line-size range is a capability statement, not a spec sheet detail.

"Drain cleaning" on its own does not tell you whether a kitchen sink or an apartment complex is in scope. A published line-size range does. Pro Jet publishes 1 inch to 12 inches and up because that is what the equipment does, and because a property manager, a restaurant owner or a general contractor deciding who to call deserves the answer before the phone call rather than after.

What the large end means in practice

An 8-inch or 12-inch line carries the output of a whole building or a whole complex, which changes three things about the job. It holds far more grease and scale before anything backs up, so when it finally does back up the volume is larger. It is usually accessed from a cleanout or manhole rather than a basement fixture. And it takes a machine that can keep pressure up in a bigger pipe, not a drum machine on a hand truck.

That work is the same crew, the same trailer-mounted unit, and the same camera as the residential side. See commercial drain and sewer service for grease programs and cast iron descaling, and real estate and multi-unit work for documentation an owner, agent or insurer can read.

Camera and locator

A high-resolution sewer camera with a locator, and you keep the footage.

The camera is pushed through the line and the locator picks the camera head up from the surface, so a defect on the monitor becomes a mark on the ground with a depth. The customer receives the footage and the marked location. That is the deliverable, not a verbal summary of what somebody saw on a screen in a van.

How camera inspection works

Two things follow from that, and both of them matter more than the camera itself.

The recommendation is checkable. If we say a line needs jetting, you can watch the deposit. If we say a section needs replacing, you can watch the break. Nobody has to take a word for it, and nothing gets recommended before the camera has been in the pipe.

The finding survives the visit. Footage and a marked location are the things you hand to an owner, a buyer, an insurer or a contractor months later. A line that was cleared and verified on video is a different asset than a line somebody says was cleared.

The camera also goes back in after a cleaning, so the file ends on a clear, full-diameter pipe rather than on the technician's say-so.

Excavation

Pro Jet digs. We do not stop at the diagnosis.

When the camera finds a collapse, a crack, an offset joint, a belly or clay tile that has come apart, Pro Jet excavates it, removes the failed pipe and installs new pipe. The diagnosis and the repair are the same company, so there is no second contractor to find.

It changes the economics of the whole job. Because the camera and locator have already given the exact spot and depth, the dig is targeted at the damage rather than opening the full run. And because the same company that found it is the one quoting the fix, the price comes off a located finding instead of a guess.

No cleaning interval fixes a shape problem. A pipe that has lost its slope, its bottom or its alignment is not a deposit, and jetting it again next year is money spent on the symptom. Sewer line repair and replacement has the full detail.

Cable machines

Sometimes a cable is the right tool, and we will tell you that.

A cable machine punches a path through a blockage. A jetter scours the whole pipe wall. Those are different jobs, and the more expensive one is not automatically the right one. Snaking is $150 to $300. Hydro jetting is $450 to $800. Both are published on the pricing page.

When we cable

  • A single, one-time blockage rather than a line that closes back up every few months.
  • A small fixture branch where a cable reaches the problem directly and nothing else in the line is wrong.
  • An object in the line, where cutting is not the point and retrieval or breaking it through is.
  • A line with no accessible cleanout or no water source to support jetting.

When we jet instead

  • The same clog keeps coming back, which means the wall was never cleaned.
  • Grease, soap and sludge are coating the pipe rather than plugging one point in it.
  • Hardened scale in cast iron is narrowing the line.
  • Small roots have come in through a joint.

The honest version of this trade-off is on the drain cleaning page with the side-by-side comparison.

Equipment and capability FAQ

What size lines can Pro Jet jet?
Pro Jet jets line sizes from 1 inch to 12 inches and up. The small end covers fixture and branch lines inside a building. The large end is commercial, multi-unit and collection-system scale: a 12-inch line is not a residential line, and Pro Jet jets them.
What pressure does the jetter run?
Pro Jet runs a trailer-mounted hydro jetter rated up to 4,000 PSI. Pressure is sized to the line rather than run flat out on everything.
Does Pro Jet handle commercial and multi-unit buildings, or only houses?
All three. Residential, commercial and multi-unit work are all Pro Jet, and the 1 inch to 12 inch and up line-size range is what makes that possible. Restaurants, commercial kitchens, retail, warehouses, industrial facilities, apartments and multi-family buildings are served with the same equipment.
Do I get the camera footage?
Yes. The sewer camera is high resolution and carries a locator, and the customer receives the footage and the marked location. That is the deliverable, not a verbal summary of what the technician saw.
When is snaking the right tool instead of jetting?
A cable machine is the right call on a single soft or object blockage in a small line, on a fixture branch where a cable reaches the problem directly, and where there is no cleanout or water source to support jetting. Snaking is $150 to $300. Hydro jetting is $450 to $800.
If the camera finds broken pipe, does Pro Jet dig it up?
Yes. Pro Jet excavates, removes the failed pipe and installs new pipe. Diagnosis is not where the job ends, and there is no second contractor to find.
How long does a job take?
Most jobs take 45 to 90 minutes. Line size, access, and whether the line has to be cleared enough for the camera to see all move a job inside that window.
Are you available on holidays?
Yes. Pro Jet runs 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, 365 days a year, including Thanksgiving, Christmas Eve and Christmas Day. There is no holiday shutdown and no separate holiday schedule.
