How it runs

Four steps, on a contract timeline.

The order matters. Nothing gets recommended before the camera has been in the pipe, and nothing gets called finished before the camera has been back.

See how camera inspection works
  1. Scope the line. A self-levelling camera on a push cable goes in through the cleanout and runs toward the city main with a footage counter going. A locator picks the camera head up from the surface, so a finding on the monitor becomes a mark in the yard with a depth. If the line is already blocked, it gets cleared enough to see first, and we say so in writing rather than calling a half-read line clear.
  2. Hand over the finding. Video, plus a written note per defect with its footage distance, its depth and its type, plus the marked location. Then the sentence everything turns on: whether this is a cleaning problem or a repair problem, and why.
  3. Clean it, if that is what it is. Grease, soap, scale and soft buildup are deposits. Hydro jetting scours the wall back toward full diameter, and the camera goes back in afterwards so the file shows a clear, full-size pipe. Drain cleaning rates are published: snaking $150–$300, hydro jetting $450–$800.
  4. Repair it, if that is what it is. An offset joint, a crack, a belly, a collapsed section of clay tile or a cast iron line that has lost its bottom is a shape problem, and no cleaning interval fixes it. Sewer line repair and full replacement with excavation is our own crew and our own equipment, quoted from the located finding rather than from a guess.

Why agents keep our number

Inspection-only outfits end the report with a line telling you to hire a plumber. That sentence arrives with days left in the inspection period, and now two parties are hunting for a repair bid against a deadline. We run the camera and we do the repair, so the finding and the price for fixing it come out of the same visit.

And one thing we do not do

We do not pay for referrals. No referral fees, no commissions, no gift cards, no spiffs, nothing of value for sending us business, and no paid preferred-vendor arrangement. That is a line we hold on purpose, and it is the right side of the rules that govern how licensees and settlement service providers can be compensated.

What we offer an agent is narrower and more useful: a camera in the pipe, a written finding you can attach to a request for repairs, and a crew that can actually do the work before closing. If a vendor is offering you something else, that is worth a second look.

Call as early in the window as you can and we will tell you straight what is possible on that date. We do not publish a turnaround guarantee, because access, weather and the schedule on the day are real. What we will do is give you an honest answer on the phone instead of an optimistic one.
What does a sewer scope cost for a listing or a buyer?
It is quoted per property, because access, line length and whether the line has to be cleared enough to see all change the job. Call (816) 506-6243 for the number on the address.
What do we get at the end of it?
The video, a written finding for every defect with its footage distance from the access point and its depth, the location marked on the ground, and a plain statement of whether the recommendation is cleaning or repair and why.
